Business-Scoped Customer Access

A multi-location foundation for ClientFlow CRM where every employee can assist the same customers without exposing data between unrelated businesses.

Executive summary

The proposal in one sentence

Treat each subscribing Business as the security boundary, let it own multiple Locations, and share its Customers with every active employee in that business.

Business rule

An employee of Business A can see Business A’s customers, regardless of location. The same employee cannot discover or access Business B’s customers. The server determines the business from the signed-in user; the browser does not choose a trusted business ID.

Why this fits the product

  • Salons, stores, grooming businesses, and clinics commonly operate more than one location.
  • A returning customer should not need a separate profile at every branch of the same business.
  • Front-desk and service employees need the same customer context to provide consistent assistance.
  • Unrelated businesses must remain strictly isolated even when their customers share an email or phone number.

Problem & model

What the existing design could not express safely

Before

  • Company_or_Location mixed a tenant with a physical branch.
  • Customers had no complete ownership path to the signed-in user.
  • Customer_Business lacked a business foreign key.
  • Customer email, phone, and address were globally unique.
  • Appointments could not identify their location.

Proposed

  • Business is the tenant and data boundary.
  • Location belongs to a business.
  • Employee links a user to a business.
  • Customer belongs directly to one business.
  • Appointment identifies a location.

Proposed relationship map

Business ├── Locations (one or many) ├── Employees │ └── User account ├── Customers (shared across locations) └── Appointments ├── Customer ├── Employee └── Location

Value & tradeoffs

Why adopt this direction

Customer experience

A customer can visit another branch of the same business without recreating their profile or history.

Employee workflow

Any authorized employee can find the customer and continue service with consistent context.

Data isolation

Tenant-scoped queries prevent unrelated businesses from observing one another’s customer records.

Future growth

Locations become a clean foundation for appointment capacity, staff assignment, hours, and reporting.

Tradeoffs and open questions

How is a business selected at login?Dev user maps to one employee/businessResolve during real authentication design

Alternatives considered

  • Location-owned customers: rejected because customers must be shared across branches.
  • Global customer directory: rejected because it creates privacy and tenant-isolation risks.
  • Many-to-many customers and businesses: deferred because unrelated businesses should keep independent records, and the simpler ownership model is easier to secure.
Team decision

Requested review and recommended next steps

The prototype is intended to start a team design conversation—not bypass the project’s issue, specification, review, and test process.

Please confirm

  1. Tenant boundary: Business owns customer data; Location does not.
  2. Access policy: all active employees in a business may read that business’s customers.
  3. Customer identity: the same real person may have separate records at unrelated businesses.
  4. Near-term scope: approve customer list/create before appointments and advanced scheduling.
  5. Future flexibility: decide whether multi-business users and multi-location employees are required now or later.

If approved

  1. Create the official feature issue.
  2. Review and accept or revise ADR-0012.
  3. Draft the customer list/create feature specification.
  4. Reconcile the prototype with the reviewed documents.
  5. Add tenant-isolation integration tests and create-customer validation.
  6. Run pnpm test, pnpm typecheck, pnpm build, and the manual verification drill before a PR.
Recommendation: accept Business as the tenant boundary, then continue with the smallest complete vertical slice: list and create company customers with explicit cross-tenant rejection tests.
